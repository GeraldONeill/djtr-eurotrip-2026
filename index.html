Logistics & Booking Guide

Everything you need to book — flights, trains, ferries, and getting around

Dan & Jenny: Flights to Italy (Arrive May 14)

Fly into: Pisa (PSA) is the closest major airport to Cinque Terre — about 1.5 hours by train to the villages. Florence (FLR) also works but adds ~2.5 hours of train travel. Genoa (GOA) is another option (~1.5 hours by train).

Tip: Book as multi-city: US → PSA (or FLR), then VCE → US on May 30 when you fly home from Venice.

Pisa → Cinque Terre: Train from Pisa Centrale to Monterosso al Mare (direct or via La Spezia), ~1.5 hours. €10–€15/pp

Flights to Florence (FLR) — All Four

Book on: Google Flights, KAYAK, or directly with airlines. Also check Chase Sapphire travel portal for point redemptions.

Typical price range: $500–$900 round trip from most US cities (or one-way to FLR, return from DBV). $$–$$$

Tip: May is shoulder season — one of the cheapest months to fly to Italy. Book ASAP for best fares. Consider multi-city booking: fly into Florence (FLR), out of Venice (VCE) for Dan & Jenny, and out of Dubrovnik (DBV) for you and Rory.

For D/J return: Dan & Jenny will fly home from Venice (VCE) on May 30. Book them a separate one-way or multi-city itinerary.

Florence → Dolomites (Bolzano/Ortisei) — Train

Route: Florence SMN → Bolzano (Trenitalia Frecciarossa or Italo), then local bus/train to Ortisei in Val Gardena (~45 min).

Duration: ~3.5 hours express to Bolzano, +45 min to Ortisei.

Price: €27–€60 per person on high-speed trains when booked in advance. $

Book on: Trainline or Trenitalia. Book 2–3 months ahead for best prices.

Alternative: Rent a car in Florence (~$50–80/day) for the flexibility to stop in Verona en route and drive into the Dolomites. Parking in Ortisei is manageable. This gives you freedom in the mountains. $$

Dolomites → Venice — Train

Route: Bus from Ortisei → Bolzano, then Bolzano → Venezia Santa Lucia (Trenitalia/Italo).

Duration: ~3.5–4 hours total.

Price: €20–€50 per person. $

Book on: Same platforms — Trainline or Trenitalia.

If you rented a car: Drop it off in Venice at Piazzale Roma (edge of the city). From there, take the vaporetto (water bus) to your hotel area. There are no cars inside Venice.

Dan & Jenny: Venice → Home — Flight (May 30)

Airport: Venice Marco Polo (VCE). Water taxi from the city center to the airport is an unforgettable experience (~€120 for a private taxi, ~€15/pp on the Alilaguna water bus). $–$$$

Tip: Book their return flight as part of a multi-city itinerary (US → FLR, VCE → US) for the best price.

Tre & Rory: Venice → Split, Croatia — Flight (May 30)

Route: VCE → SPU. EasyJet, Volotea, and Croatia Airlines fly this route in summer. You're flying the same morning Dan & Jenny fly home — head to the airport together, say your goodbyes at the gate.

Duration: ~1 hour flight.

Price: €30–€80 per person one-way on budget carriers. $

Book on: Google Flights or direct with EasyJet. Try to get a midday or early afternoon departure so you arrive in Split with daylight.

Split → Hvar — Catamaran Ferry (June 1)

Operators: Jadrolinija, Kapetan Luka (KRILO), TP Line.

Duration: ~1 hour by high-speed catamaran.

Price: €10–€25 per person. $

Frequency: Up to 10+ daily departures in summer. Morning departures recommended.

Book on: Ferryhopper or CroatiaFerries.com. Book at least a few days ahead in summer.

Hvar → Dubrovnik — Catamaran Ferry (June 4)

Operators: KRILO (departs ~8:50 AM, 3h 15min), TP Line (departs ~10:45 AM, 3h 50min), Jadrolinija (afternoon).

Price: €25–€40 per person. $

Book on: Same platforms. Book early — this is one of Croatia's busiest routes.

Tre & Rory: Dubrovnik → Home — Flight (June 7)

Airport: Dubrovnik (DBV). Small airport, ~25 min from Old Town by bus or taxi.

Price: Varies. Consider booking as multi-city: US → FLR / DBV → US.

Chase Sapphire: Check the Chase travel portal for point redemptions on the return leg — Dubrovnik is well connected via European hubs (London, Munich, Amsterdam) for connecting flights home.

Prologue: Cinque Terre

May 14–22  |  Dan & Jenny

Dan & Jenny's Adventure

Nine days on the Ligurian coast before the family portion begins. The plan: base yourself in one of the five villages, hike the legendary coastal trails village to village, eat extraordinary seafood, explore medieval churches and fortresses, then work your way toward Florence via Portovenere, the Gulf of Poets, and Lucca. Every day has a hike. Every evening has a view. This is your trip.

Thu, May 14 Arrive in Cinque Terre Dan & Jenny

Land in Pisa, train to the coast. By evening you'll be sitting on a terrace above the Mediterranean with a plate of fresh anchovies and a glass of Ligurian white wine. Jet lag never felt this good.

  • Fly into Pisa (PSA). From Pisa Centrale station, take the train to Monterosso al Mare (~1.5 hours, direct or via La Spezia). Trains run frequently €10–€15/pp
  • Check in, settle, explore Monterosso. The largest of the five villages, split into the old town (medieval, charming, car-free) and new town (beach, more restaurants). Walk through the tunnel between them. The old town has the 13th-century Church of San Giovanni Battista with its striking striped marble facade
  • Buy a Cinque Terre Card at the train station or park office. The Trekking Card (~€16/day) includes all trail access. The Train+Trekking Card (~€20/day) adds unlimited train rides between the five villages — get this one, it's worth it €20/day or multi-day passes available
  • Evening walk: Stroll along Monterosso's seafront promenade. The statue of Il Gigante (a 14th-century stone giant holding a terrace on his shoulders) is at the far end of the beach — worth seeking out
  • Dinner: Ristorante Miky — serving some of the finest seafood in Cinque Terre since 1980. Chef Miky De Fina's acciugata alla Monterossina (anchovy spaghetti) is the signature dish. Reserve ahead $$$
  • Gelato: Gelateria Golosone — widely considered the best gelato in all of Cinque Terre
Fri, May 15 Hike: Monterosso → Vernazzo Dan & Jenny

The most challenging section of the Blue Trail (Sentiero Azzurro) — and the most rewarding. Do it first while you're fresh. Steep climbs, narrow paths, and then Vernazzo appears below you like a painting.

  • Morning: Hike Monterosso → Vernazzo (Trail #2, Sentiero Azzurro). ~3.5 km, 1.5–2 hours. Rated strenuous — persistent vertical gain, narrow with high-exposure moments, and lots of stone steps. The views are extraordinary: terraced vineyards dropping into the sea, the coastline stretching in both directions. Bring water and sturdy shoes Included with Cinque Terre Card
  • Arrive in Vernazzo — arguably the most beautiful of the five villages. A natural harbor enclosed by colorful houses stacked up the hillside, with the medieval Doria Castle watching over everything. Walk down to the harbor piazza and take it in
  • Lunch: Ristorante Belforte — inside an 11th-century fortress overlooking the harbor. Spaghetti with mussels and anchovies, eaten in a medieval tower above the Mediterranean. One of those meals you don't forget $$$
  • Afternoon: Explore Vernazzo. Climb up to Doria Castle for panoramic views of the village and coastline €2. Visit the Church of Santa Margherita d'Antiochia (14th century, built on a rocky promontory). Wander the narrow caruggi (alleyways)
  • Late afternoon: Find a bench at the harbor with a focaccia di Recco (cheese-stuffed flatbread, a Ligurian specialty) and watch the fishing boats
  • Train back to your base (~4 min to Monterosso, covered by your card). Or if you're based in Vernazzo, you're home
  • Dinner: Il Pirata delle Cinque Terre — charming eatery run by twin Sicilian brothers. Friendly atmosphere, great local wine, excellent seafood. Or Lunch Box Vernazzo for incredible sandwiches if you want casual $$
Sat, May 16 Hike: Vernazzo → Corniglia Dan & Jenny

Another spectacular coastal hike, slightly easier than yesterday. Corniglia is the only village not at sea level — it sits on a hilltop ridge 100m above the water, surrounded by vineyards. The quietest, most authentic of the five.

  • Morning: Hike Vernazzo → Corniglia (Trail #2). ~4 km, 1.5–2 hours. Medium difficulty with the highest point on the entire Blue Trail. Steep stone steps but incredible views — terraced hillsides, olive groves, and the sea far below
  • Arrive in Corniglia. (If you took the train, you'll climb the famous Lardarina — 382 brick steps from the station to the village. Or take the shuttle bus.) The village sits on a promontory with 360-degree views. It's the smallest, quietest, and most wine-focused of the five towns
  • Explore Corniglia: Walk the main street (Via Fieschi), find the 14th-century Church of San Pietro with its stunning rose window. Continue to the terrace at the end of Via Fieschi for a panoramic viewpoint that's one of the best in all of Cinque Terre
  • Lunch: Try the local wine — Corniglia produces excellent Sciacchetrà, a sweet dessert wine made from dried grapes on the terraces. Pair it with local cheese and focaccia at a small bar on Via Fieschi $–$$
  • Afternoon: Optional extended hike — if you have energy, take the trail from Corniglia up to Volastra (a tiny hilltop hamlet above Manarola) through ancient terraced vineyards. About 1 hour, moderate. Or relax and train back to base
  • Dinner at your base. If Monterosso: L'Ancora della Tortuga — cliff-edge terrace, candlelit, seafood. Romantic and special $$$
Sun, May 17 Hike: Corniglia → Manarola Dan & Jenny

The prettiest section of the Blue Trail — sea views the entire way, passing through Mediterranean gardens and olive groves. Manarola might be the most photogenic village in the world.

  • Morning: Train to Corniglia, then hike Corniglia → Manarola (Trail #2). ~3 km, ~1 hour 15 min. The easiest section of the Blue Trail — relatively gentle with spectacular gardens and sea views the entire way
  • Arrive in Manarola. The village tumbles down a steep ravine to a tiny harbor carved into the rock. Every angle is a postcard. This is the most photographed village in Cinque Terre for good reason
  • Explore Manarola: The Church of San Lorenzo (1338) has a beautiful rose window. Walk down to the harbor and watch the boats. Climb up to the cemetery above town for an incredible view looking back at the village
  • Lunch: Trattoria Dal Billy — the most celebrated restaurant in Manarola, perched high above the village with stunning views. The seafood starter features 12 different small plates of fresh seafood. The handmade taglierini with shrimp and pine nuts is legendary. Reserve ahead — it books up. Wear good shoes, it's a climb to get there $$$
  • Afternoon: Nessun Dorma — the most famous aperitivo bar in Cinque Terre, with what may be the best sunset view on the entire Italian coast. Bruschetta, anchovy platters, Ligurian wine, and that view. Arrive early for a seat $$
  • Train back to base after sunset. The trains run until ~11 PM
Mon, May 18 Via dell'Amore & Riomaggiore Dan & Jenny

The legendary Via dell'Amore (Path of Love) just reopened in 2025 after years of renovation. You walk it today. Then explore Riomaggiore — the southern bookend of the five villages.

  • Morning: Walk the Via dell'Amore (Manarola → Riomaggiore). ~1 km, ~20 minutes. The most famous path in Cinque Terre — flat, paved, carved into the cliff face above the sea. It reopened on Valentine's Day 2025 after 12 years of closure. Reservation required — book through the Cinque Terre National Park website. There's a supplemental fee on top of the Cinque Terre Card €10 supplement + card
  • Arrive in Riomaggiore — the southernmost village. Houses painted in every color cascade down a steep valley to a tiny harbor. Walk down to the marina where fishing boats are winched up the concrete ramp
  • Explore Riomaggiore: The Castle of Riomaggiore (13th century) has free entry and good views. The Church of San Giovanni Battista (1340) sits at the top of the village. Walk the narrow main street and peek into small art galleries
  • Lunch: Grab a fritto misto di mare (fried mixed seafood cone) from one of the takeaway shops on the main street and eat it overlooking the harbor. This is the quintessential Cinque Terre lunch $
  • Afternoon option: Hike to the Santuario di Montenero — a trail from Riomaggiore climbs to this hilltop sanctuary (~1.5 hours round trip, moderate). Panoramic views of the entire Cinque Terre coastline and out to the islands. A beautiful, less-crowded hike
  • Dinner: Dau Cila — right on the harbor in Riomaggiore, fresh seafood, candlelit, water lapping below. Or Rio Bistrot for a more modern take on Ligurian cuisine $$–$$$
Tue, May 19 Portovenere & the Gulf of Poets Dan & Jenny

Beyond the five villages lies Portovenere — a UNESCO World Heritage site where pastel houses line a natural harbor and a dramatic Gothic church perches on a rocky cliff. Byron, Shelley, and D.H. Lawrence all wrote here. You can see why.

  • Morning: Ferry from Riomaggiore (or La Spezia) to Portovenere — ~30 min from Riomaggiore, longer from other villages. The boat ride along the coast is gorgeous. Alternatively, take the bus from La Spezia (~30 min) €10–€18/pp ferry
  • Explore Portovenere: Walk the colorful waterfront of Calata Doria. Visit the Church of San Pietro — a stunning black-and-white striped Gothic church built on a rocky promontory at the edge of the harbor, dating to the 6th century. The views from here are extraordinary
  • Climb to Doria Castle — the massive Genoese fortress above town. Panoramic views of the harbor, the islands, and the coastline. Small museum inside €5
  • Visit Byron's Grotto (Grotta di Byron) — a sea cave at the base of the cliffs where Lord Byron reportedly swam. The path down is dramatic
  • Lunch at the harbor: Fresh seafood on the waterfront — focaccia, pesto (this is Liguria, birthplace of pesto), and local white wine. Try muscles stuffed with breadcrumbs, a Portovenere specialty $$
  • Afternoon hike option: Portovenere → Riomaggiore trail (Trail AV5T/SVA). This is a serious hike — ~3 hours, steep initial ascent, but spectacular ridgeline views of the entire coast. Only attempt if you're feeling strong. Otherwise, take the ferry back
  • Alternative: Boat to Palmaria Island — the largest island in the Gulf, with hiking trails, swimming coves, and the Blue Grotto (sea cave). Ferries run frequently from Portovenere harbor €5–€8 ferry
  • Dinner back at base or in La Spezia if you're passing through — Osteria della Corte in La Spezia is excellent for a more local, non-tourist dinner $$
Wed, May 20 Lerici & the Coastal Path Dan & Jenny

The other side of the Gulf of Poets. Lerici is a gorgeous seaside town with a massive medieval castle, a palm-lined promenade, and a coastal hiking path to the neighboring village of Tellaro — one of Italy's most beautiful small towns. Fewer tourists, same beauty.

  • Morning: Bus or ferry from La Spezia to Lerici (~20 min by bus, ~40 min by ferry). The ferry gives you views of the entire Gulf of Poets
  • Explore Lerici: The Castello di Lerici is a massive 12th-century fortress with a museum and panoramic views of the gulf. Inside the castle, there's a chapel with medieval frescoes. The town itself is an elegant resort with pastel buildings, a sandy beach, and a lovely promenade €5 castle entry
  • Hike: Lerici → Tellaro (coastal path, ~1.5 hours, easy-moderate). A beautiful walk along the coast through Mediterranean scrubland, past hidden coves and swimming spots. Tellaro is a tiny fishing village clinging to a cliff — no cars, no tourists, just stone houses, a church on the rocks, and one of Italy's prettiest piazzas
  • Lunch in Tellaro: Locanda Miranda or Il Frantoio — tiny local restaurants with fresh catch of the day. Eat on a terrace overlooking the sea in this impossibly charming village $$
  • Afternoon: Swim at one of the coves between Lerici and Tellaro if the weather is warm. Or explore Tellaro at leisure — it's the kind of place where you just sit and exist
  • Return to Lerici (walk or small boat). Take the bus or ferry back to La Spezia, then train to your base
  • Dinner: Celebrate your last night in Cinque Terre. If in Monterosso: Enoteca Eliseo — intimate wine bar with Ligurian small plates, local bottles. Perfect final evening $$
  • Pack up. Tomorrow you leave the coast for a Renaissance city detour
Thu, May 21 Lucca: The Walled City Dan & Jenny

Instead of going straight to Florence, stop in Lucca — a perfectly preserved Renaissance city enclosed by 16th-century walls you can walk and bike on top of. It's on the way, it's gorgeous, and it's one of Tuscany's best-kept secrets. Dan, you're going to love the architecture.

  • Train: La Spezia → Lucca (~1.5 hours, change in Pisa or Viareggio). Drop your bags at your accommodation
  • Walk (or bike) the city walls. Lucca's 16th-century walls are 4 km around, tree-lined, and wide enough for pedestrians and cyclists. They're the best-preserved Renaissance fortification walls in Italy. Walking the full circuit is the perfect orientation — you see the entire city from above. Rent bikes if you prefer Free to walk / €5–€10 bike rental
  • Explore the historic center: The Piazza dell'Anfiteatro — a perfect oval piazza built on top of a Roman amphitheater, ringed with cafes. The Cathedral of San Martino (11th century) with its asymmetrical facade and the Volto Santo (a revered wooden crucifix said to be carved by Nicodemus). Torre Guinigi — a medieval tower with live oak trees growing on top. Climb it for 360-degree views €5 tower
  • Lunch: Trattoria da Leo — beloved local trattoria, no-frills, packed with Lucchesi. Handmade pasta, soups, and the local specialty tordelli lucchesi (large meat-stuffed pasta). Cash preferred $
  • Afternoon: Visit the Church of San Michele in Foro — a Romanesque masterpiece with a facade of ornate columns, each one different. The Palazzo Pfanner gardens are peaceful and beautiful €4.50. If time allows, the National Museum of Villa Guinigi has excellent medieval and Renaissance art
  • Evening walk: Lucca is magical at dusk. The piazzas empty out, the trattorias light up, and the walls glow in the golden hour
  • Dinner: Buca di Sant'Antonio — Lucca's most celebrated restaurant, open since 1782. Traditional Lucchese cuisine: farro soup, roast rabbit, chestnut flour desserts. Musical instruments and copper pots hang from the ceiling. Reserve ahead $$–$$$
Fri, May 22 Arrive Florence — Waiting for the Kids Dan & Jenny

Short train to Florence, and now you have a free day in the city before Tre and Rory arrive tomorrow. Get a head start on the Renaissance.

  • Train: Lucca → Florence — ~1.5 hours (regional train to Firenze SMN). Easy, scenic ride through the Tuscan hills €8–€12/pp
  • Check in to your Florence accommodation (same place where you'll stay with the whole group starting tomorrow)
  • Afternoon: Get oriented. Walk the historic center — cross the Ponte Vecchio, stroll along the Arno, find the Duomo and just admire it from the piazza. No museums today — save those for when the whole family is together. Just absorb the city
  • Optional: San Miniato al Monte — a beautiful Romanesque church up the hill past Piazzale Michelangelo. Fewer crowds than the piazzale, equally stunning views, and the church interior (11th century) is extraordinary. The Benedictine monks sell honey and herbal liqueurs in the gift shop Free
  • Dinner: Trattoria Sergio Gozzi — a fourth-generation family trattoria near the Mercato Centrale, open since 1915. Lunch-only most days, but check evening hours. Alternatively, Trattoria Sostanza — might as well get a preview of the butter chicken before the kids arrive $$
  • Evening walk: Cross the Ponte Vecchio at night. Walk up to Piazzale Michelangelo for the sunset view over the city. Toast to your nine days on the coast and to the family adventure starting tomorrow

Part 1: Italy

May 23–29  |  Tre, Rory, Dan & Jenny

Sat, May 23 Arrive in Florence All Four

Land in Florence, get settled, ease into the city. Don't fight the jet lag — lean into the evening energy of Florence instead.

  • Get to your hotel/apartment, unpack, freshen up. If you arrive early enough, take a walk to get oriented — Florence's historic center is compact and walkable
  • First stop: Piazzale Michelangelo for the iconic panoramic sunset view over the city — the Duomo, Ponte Vecchio, the Arno, the hills. Best first impression of Florence you can get. It's a 15-min walk uphill from the center, or a quick taxi Free
  • Dinner: Trattoria Sostanza — a cozy, old-school single-room trattoria. Order the butter chicken and the artichoke omelet. Cash only, no reservations, arrive by 7:30 PM or you're waiting. It's been open since 1869 and hasn't changed $$
  • Alternative dinner: Trattoria Mario — communal tables, pure Florentine cooking, loud and energetic. Lunch-only normally, but worth knowing about for tomorrow $
  • After dinner: Walk over the Ponte Vecchio at night (it's gorgeous lit up), grab gelato at Vivoli or La Carraia, and call it a night. Jenny — perfect first night: low energy, high reward
Sun, May 24 Renaissance Day All Four

This is Dan's day. Florence is the birthplace of the Renaissance, and this is where you soak it in — the Uffizi, the Duomo, the David. Build the day around art and history, with food and wine filling the gaps.

  • Morning: Uffizi Gallery — Botticelli's Birth of Venus, Caravaggio, Leonardo, Raphael. Book timed-entry tickets in advance (they sell out). Go early to beat the crowds. Allow 2–3 hours €20–€25/pp
  • Mid-morning break: Coffee and a pastry at a cafe near the Piazza della Signoria. Sit outside, people-watch, absorb the open-air sculpture gallery that is the piazza itself
  • Late morning: The Duomo complex — the cathedral is free to enter. The Brunelleschi's Dome climb (463 steps) has insane views but is strenuous — Dan, Tre, and Rory could do this while Jenny explores the Baptistery and the museum at ground level. Or everyone skips the climb and just admires from the piazza €30/pp for dome + museum combo
  • Lunch: Mercato Centrale (Central Market) — the upstairs food hall has incredible stalls: lampredotto sandwiches, fresh pasta, truffled everything, local wine by the glass. Casual, delicious, everyone picks what they want $–$$
  • Afternoon: Galleria dell'Accademia — home of Michelangelo's David. Book tickets in advance. It's smaller than the Uffizi so 1–1.5 hours is plenty €16/pp
  • Late afternoon: Stroll through the leather market at San Lorenzo — great for gifts, belts, bags, journals. Haggle a little
  • Dinner: Buca Mario — Florentine bistecca (T-bone steak), rustic cellar atmosphere, one of the oldest restaurants in Florence (1886). Share the bistecca alla fiorentina for the table. Jenny can enjoy the pastas and sides — incredible ribollita $$$
  • Wine after dinner: Le Volpi e l'Uva — tiny wine bar near Ponte Vecchio, curated selection of small Italian producers. A few sips for Jenny, full pours for the rest $$
  • Amaro nightcap: Ask for a digestivo wherever you end up — Florence is the spiritual home of Amaro. Try Amaro del Capo (Calabrian, herbal) or Amaro Santoni (a rare, truly local Florentine Amaro that's hard to find even in Italy). Also ask about Amaro dell'Erborista — small-batch, family-recipe Amari from Tuscan herbalists. Many trattorias will have their own house Amaro made from secret recipes. Always ask "avete un amaro della casa?"
Mon, May 25 Oltrarno, Artisans & Tuscan Vibes All Four

Cross the Arno to the Oltrarno — Florence's bohemian left bank. This is the artisan quarter: leather workshops, paper marbling studios, quiet piazzas with real Florentine neighborhood energy. And this afternoon: you're making pasta together.

  • Morning: Wander the Oltrarno neighborhood — peek into artisan workshops along Via Maggio and Borgo San Frediano. You'll see bookbinders, leather artisans, and woodworkers at work. Stop at Giulio Giannini e Figlio for hand-marbled paper goods (open since 1856) Free to browse
  • Optional: Palazzo Pitti & Boboli Gardens — the Medici grand palace with the Palatine Gallery (Raphael, Titian, Caravaggio) and sprawling Renaissance gardens. Dan will love this €16–€22/pp

Book through Airbnb Experiences or Cookly — look for small-group classes (6–8 people max) with a local nonna or chef. This is the family activity of the trip. You'll eat what you make, and they usually include wine pairings €60–€90/pp
  • Dinner: You'll likely be full from the cooking class, so keep it light — grab a seat at Piazza Santo Spirito for an Aperol Spritz and snacks, or if you're hungry: Il Latini — boisterous, family-style Tuscan feast with hams hanging from the ceiling $$–$$$
  • Evening — Amaro crawl: This is the night for it. Head to COMPANION Dolceamaro Bar (Via Palazzuolo) — a traditional Italian bar dedicated to the art of Amaro, with an extensive collection of family-recipe digestivi from all over Italy. Ask the bartender to walk you through regional Amari — every region of Italy has its own, and many are guarded family recipes passed down for generations. Then try Manifattura — their cocktail menu celebrates indigenous Italian liquor heritage, including Amaro Santoni (a rare Florentine Amaro). Jenny can take little tastes — Amaro is served in small pours anyway $$
Tue, May 26 Travel Day: Into the Mountains All Four

Trade Renaissance palaces for Alpine peaks. Today you travel from Florence to the Dolomites. The train ride through the Italian countryside is gorgeous — watch Tuscany fade into the Alps.

Wed, May 27 Dolomites: Peaks & Panoramas All Four

The big mountain day — and the group splits for the morning. Tre and Rory hit the trails on bikes. Dan and Jenny take the gondola and hike. Everyone regroups for a rifugio lunch in the sky.

  • Tre & Rory — Morning: Mountain biking in Val Gardena. The Trail Arena Val Gardena is a dedicated bike park with 6 groomed flow trails, freeride tracks, and 13.9 km of lift-accessed riding connected by gondolas. Important: the Trail Arena typically opens mid-June, so it may not be fully open yet on May 27. However, the valley's broader 600 km network of mountain bike trails is rideable by late May — enduro, all-mountain, and cross-country routes through the Dolomites. Rent full-suspension enduro bikes (150–170mm travel) from Intersport Val Gardena or local shops in Ortisei. Tell them you want to ride the MTB trails — they'll set you up and point you to the best routes for conditions €40–€70/day bike rental, lift pass extra if open
  • Dan & Jenny — Morning: Seceda Gondola + Ridgeline Hike. Take the gondola from Ortisei directly to the top of Seceda (2,500m). The ridgeline walk is one of the most photographed views in all the Dolomites: jagged spires dropping away into green valleys. It's a 4-hour, two-hut, two-beer trek along a stunning plateau — perfect for Dan and Jenny's hiking chops. Extend or shorten based on energy ~€35–€40/pp round trip gondola
  • Alternative hike: Alpe di Siusi (Seiser Alm) — Europe's largest high-altitude Alpine meadow. Gondola up, then gentle walks through wildflower meadows with dramatic mountain backdrops. Extremely photogenic ~€35/pp gondola
  • Regroup for lunch at a rifugio (mountain hut): There are mountain huts along the trails serving South Tyrolean food — hearty soups, speck platters, strudel, and beer. Eating at a rifugio with Dolomite peaks all around you is one of those lifetime experiences. Coordinate a meeting point — the huts near Seceda are reachable from multiple trails $–$$
  • Afternoon: Return to Ortisei, grab a coffee and cake at a cafe in the pedestrian zone. Visit a wood-carving workshop or gallery — Val Gardena's tradition goes back centuries
  • Dinner: Anna Stuben (Michelin-starred but approachable) or a traditional Stube (wood-paneled South Tyrolean dining room) at your hotel. Order the canederli (bread dumplings) and apple strudel. End with a local digestivo — South Tyrol makes its own Alpine Amari infused with mountain herbs, pine, and gentian root $$–$$$$
Thu, May 28 Mountains to Water: Arrive Venice All Four

From Alpine peaks to floating canals. The contrast is part of the magic. Travel day, but Venice rewards you immediately — the moment you step off the train and see the Grand Canal, it hits different. Guided tour this afternoon to get the deep history, then aperitivo on a canal.

  • Morning: Breakfast at the hotel, check out, bus from Ortisei → Bolzano (~45 min)
  • Train: Bolzano → Venezia Santa Lucia — ~3.5 hours. The train drops you right at the edge of Venice's Grand Canal. Step off, and you're there €20–€50/pp
  • If you rented a car: Drop it off at Piazzale Roma (parking garages at the edge of Venice, ~€25–€35/day). Then vaporetto or water taxi to your hotel
  • Getting to your hotel: Venice has no cars. You'll take a vaporetto (water bus) — Line 1 goes down the Grand Canal and is basically a sightseeing tour. Buy a 48-hour vaporetto pass €30 (48hr)
  • Afternoon: Guided walking tour. Book a small-group art and history tour — Context Travel runs expert-led walks (max 10 people) covering Renaissance art, maritime history, and hidden churches with real historians and art scholars. Or Avventure Bellissime's Best of Venice tour — St. Mark's Basilica, Doge's Palace, Bridge of Sighs, the Venetian Republic's rise and fall, all with a knowledgeable guide who'll give you the deep history you want. Dan especially will eat this up €60–€120/pp depending on group size
  • Sunset aperitivo — this is happening. Find a canal-side bar and have a proper Venetian Spritz (the original — Venice invented it). Il Caffè Rosso on Campo Santa Margherita is perfect — lively piazza, cheap drinks, great people-watching. Or Naranzaria at the Rialto for Grand Canal views with your Spritz $
  • Dinner: Osteria alle Testiere — tiny (10 tables), seafood-focused, one of the best restaurants in Venice. Reserve well in advance. Or Trattoria alla Madonna for a more traditional, bustling Venetian experience $$–$$$
  • Amaro after dinner: Venice has its own Amaro tradition — ask for Amaro Nardini (from the Veneto, made since 1779) or Select Aperitivo (the original Venetian bitter, predating Aperol). Any bacaro worth its salt will have a shelf of regional Amari. The bartender will have opinions — let them guide you
Fri, May 29 Venice: Canals, Art & the Last Supper Together All Four

Last full day together as a family. Venice is magical, overwhelming, and unlike anywhere else on Earth. Make it count — gondola, art, cicchetti, and a Michelin-starred farewell dinner.

  • Morning: St. Mark's Basilica & Piazza San Marco — the golden mosaics inside the basilica are jaw-dropping. Go early (opens 9:30 AM) to beat the crowds. Skip-the-line tickets available. The piazza itself is the "drawing room of Europe" (Napoleon said that) €3 basilica / €8 with Pala d'Oro
  • Doge's Palace — Gothic masterpiece, the seat of Venetian power for 700 years. The Bridge of Sighs connects to the prison. Dan will love the history. Book timed entry €30/pp
  • Mid-morning: Cicchetti crawl — Venice's version of tapas. Pop into bacari (wine bars) and eat small plates at the counter: baccalà mantecato, sarde in saor, polpette. The best crawl area is around the Rialto Market. Try All'Arco and Cantina Do Spade $–$$
  • Early afternoon: Gondola ride. All four of you, through the quiet back canals. Yes, it's the thing you have to do — and it earns that reputation. A ~30-min ride in late afternoon light, gliding under stone bridges past crumbling palazzi, is genuinely unforgettable. Up to 6 people per gondola so you all fit €80–€100 for the gondola
  • Afternoon: Choose your own adventure:
    • Peggy Guggenheim Collection — modern art in a Grand Canal palazzo. Picasso, Pollock, Dalí, Ernst. Wonderful museum, manageable size €16/pp
    • Murano & Burano half-day trip — take the vaporetto to Murano (glass-blowing) and Burano (rainbow-colored houses, lace-making). Covered by your vaporetto pass
  • Farewell Dinner: Ristorante Quadri — Michelin-starred, overlooking Piazza San Marco from an elegant second-floor dining room. Venetian tasting menus, impeccable service, and the view of the basilica lit up at night is extraordinary. This is the family send-off dinner. Reserve well in advance — this books up. The experience is worth the splurge $$$$
  • After dinner: Walk Venice at night together one last time. The city is hauntingly beautiful when the day-trippers leave. Cross the Rialto Bridge, follow the Grand Canal, get lost. End the night with a final round of Amari at a bacaro — Venetian Amaro Montenegro or Fernet-Branca are classic closers
Sat, May 30 Farewell Venice — Fly to Split All Four → Splitting Up

Bittersweet morning. Breakfast together, hugs, and then you all head to the airport. Dan and Jenny fly home. Tre and Rory fly to Split. By afternoon, the romantic portion begins on the Adriatic.

  • Morning: Breakfast together, one last walk. Head to the airport together — Venice Marco Polo (VCE) via Alilaguna water bus (~€15/pp, ~1.5 hrs) or private water taxi (~€120, 30 min). The water taxi through the lagoon is an unforgettable departure $–$$$
  • Dan & Jenny: Fly home from VCE. Book as multi-city itinerary (US → FLR, VCE → US)
  • Tre & Rory: Fly VCE → Split (SPU) — ~1 hour. EasyJet, Volotea, or Croatia Airlines. Try to book a midday or early afternoon flight so you arrive with time to explore €30–€80/pp
  • Arrive Split. Airport → Old Town: Bus #37 (~30 min) or taxi (~€35). Drop bags, check in
  • Afternoon: First taste of Diocletian's Palace — this isn't a ruin behind a fence. It's the actual living center of Split. 1,700 years old, with apartments, restaurants, and bars built into the Roman walls. Walk the Peristyle (the central courtyard), duck into the underground cellars €10–€15 for cathedral + cellars
  • Sunset: Walk the Riva — Split's famous waterfront promenade. Palm trees, cafe terraces, the harbor, the mountains across the water. Your first Adriatic sunset as a couple
  • Dinner: Konoba Matejuska — tiny family-run konoba (tavern) right on the harbor. Dalmatian seafood, grilled fish, local wine. No-frills, incredible quality. The perfect first night $$

Part 2: Croatia

May 30 – June 7  |  Tre & Rory

Romance Mode: Activated

This is your week. The Dalmatian Coast is one of the most beautiful coastlines in the world — ancient walled cities, turquoise water, lavender-scented islands, incredible seafood and wine, and sunsets that don't quit. The itinerary flows: Split → Hvar (island time) → Dubrovnik (grand finale). Every day has built-in romance but also adventure — you're not just sitting on a beach (unless you want to). Sun, May 31 Split: Deep Dive & Adventure Tre & Rory

Full day in Split. Morning culture, afternoon adventure. This city has more layers than you'd expect.

  • Morning: Wander the neighborhoods outside the palace walls — Varos is an old fisherman's quarter with steep stone streets and laundry lines, barely any tourists. Mon, Jun 1 Ferry to Hvar — Island Time Begins Tre & Rory

Catch the morning catamaran to Hvar. One hour across turquoise water, and you step off onto one of the most beautiful islands in the world (literally — Condé Nast said so). Hvar Town is lavender, stone, and light.

  • Morning catamaran: Split → Hvar Town — ~1 hour. KRILO, Jadrolinija, or TP Line. Buy tickets on Ferryhopper a few days ahead €10–€25/pp
  • Check in, explore Hvar Town: Drop your bags, then walk the main square (one of the largest and most beautiful in all of Dalmatia). Wander the marble streets, peek into churches, climb the fortress above town for panoramic views
  • Afternoon: Climb to the Spanish Fortress (Fortica) — 15-min walk up from town. The views of the harbor, the Pakleni Islands, and the open Adriatic are staggering. Best in late afternoon light €8/pp
  • Beach time: Walk or water taxi to the Pakleni Islands — a chain of tiny forested islands just offshore with hidden coves, beach bars, and crystal water. Palmizana is the most popular (art gallery + restaurant + beach) €10–€15 water taxi
  • Dinner: Gariful — right on the harbor, lobster and seafood with boats bobbing beside your table. Splurge night. Or Dalmatino — romantic stone courtyard, locally caught fish, excellent Hvar wines $$$

Tue, Jun 2 Hvar: Lavender, Wine & Sea Tre & Rory

The interior of Hvar Island is rolling hills of lavender, ancient vineyards, and abandoned stone villages. Today is about exploring beyond the town — rent a scooter or car and see the island.

  • Rent a scooter or small car for the day — this is the best way to explore. The island is ~70 km long with one main road and tiny side roads leading to hidden beaches and villages €30–€50 scooter / €50–€80 car
  • Drive to Stari Grad — the oldest town in Croatia (2,400 years old, founded by Greeks). The Stari Grad Plain is a UNESCO World Heritage Site — ancient agricultural plots still in use, divided by stone walls built by Greek colonists. Surreal
  • Wine tasting: Stop at a winery in the interior — Hvar produces excellent wines, especially the indigenous red Plavac Mali. Tomić Winery near Jelsa is a great option — cellar tour + tasting with vineyard views €15–€25/pp
  • Lunch at a konoba in the interior: Find a tiny family-run tavern in one of the hilltop villages. Grilled lamb, local cheese, homemade bread, house wine. This is the real Croatia $–$$
  • Afternoon: Hidden beach hunt — the south side of the island has dozens of secluded coves. Dubovica is one of the most beautiful — a pebble beach in a turquoise bay surrounded by pines. Bring a towel and snacks
  • Return to Hvar Town for sunset. Hula Hula again for sunset drinks — it never gets old

Wed, Jun 3 Hvar: Beach Day & Romance Tre & Rory

Low-key day before the ferry to Dubrovnik tomorrow. Beach, swim, eat, repeat. This is your recharge day.

Thu, Jun 4 Ferry to Dubrovnik — The Grand Finale Tre & Rory

The catamaran to Dubrovnik is a journey in itself — 3+ hours down the Dalmatian Coast, passing islands, fishing villages, and eventually the unmistakable walled city rising from the sea.

  • Morning catamaran: Hvar → Dubrovnik — KRILO departs ~8:50 AM (3h 15min), TP Line ~10:45 AM (3h 50min). Book in advance €25–€40/pp
  • Arrive early afternoon. Check in, freshen up. Your hotel/apartment should be in or very near the Old Town walls
  • Afternoon: Explore the Old Town on foot.   • Sunset drinks: Buža Bar — the famous "hole in the wall" cliff bar. You literally walk through a gap in the city walls and you're on a terrace carved into the cliffs above the sea. Cold beer, jaw-dropping views. There are two Buža bars; Bar II is the bigger, more famous one $–$$

Fri, Jun 5 Dubrovnik: Island, Views & Explore Tre & Rory

First full day in Dubrovnik. Lokrum Island in the morning, cable car for views in the afternoon, and a great dinner to set the stage for tomorrow's grand finale.

  • Morning: Lokrum Island — a 15-min ferry from the Old Port. A car-free island nature reserve with botanical gardens, a medieval monastery, peacocks walking around, a saltwater lake ("Dead Sea") perfect for swimming, and rocky coves. Pack swimsuits €20/pp ferry round trip (includes island entry)

Sat, Jun 6 Dubrovnik: Grand Finale Tre & Rory

Your last full day of the entire trip. Walk the city walls in the morning golden light, then spend the afternoon at the beach and the evening at your most romantic dinner yet.

  • Morning: Walk the City Walls — the #1 thing to do in Dubrovnik. A ~2 km circuit around the entire Old Town on top of medieval fortifications. Views of terracotta rooftops, the harbor, the Adriatic, and Lokrum Island. Go first thing when it opens to beat the heat and crowds €35/pp
  • Late morning: Sea kayaking around the walls — paddle underneath the city walls, around Lokrum, into sea caves. Guided tours include snorkeling stops. Seeing the walls from the water after walking on top of them is a completely different perspective €35–€50/pp

  • Golden hour: Buža Bar one final time — cold beers on the cliff as the sun sets behind the Adriatic. This is the moment
  • Grand finale dinner: Nautika — Dubrovnik's most celebrated restaurant, perched on the city walls at the Pile Gate entrance. Seafood tasting menus, white tablecloths, fortress views. The most romantic dinner setting in the country. Or Proto — upstairs terrace, Dubrovnik's oldest fish restaurant (1886), candlelit, cathedral views $$$–$$$$

Depending on your flight time, you may have a relaxed morning. Dubrovnik's airport is small and close — don't stress.

Quick Tips

  • Book museums and restaurants NOW. Uffizi, Accademia, and the Duomo dome climb sell out weeks ahead. Same for top restaurants — Osteria alle Testiere, Trattoria Sostanza, and Nautika all need reservations
  • Trains in Italy: Book on Trainline or Trenitalia 2–3 months ahead for the best prices. Frecciarossa (red) is the fast train. Italo (maroon) is the competing fast train. Both are great
  • Croatia ferries: Book catamaran tickets on Ferryhopper at least a week ahead in summer. They fill up, especially Split→Hvar and Hvar→Dubrovnik
  • Money: Italy and Croatia both use the Euro. Cards are accepted almost everywhere, but carry some cash for market vendors, small trattorias, and tips
  • Chase Sapphire: Use it for flights (transfer points to airline partners for better value), hotels via the Chase travel portal, and as your primary card abroad — no foreign transaction fees, trip protection built in
  • Water: Tap water is safe to drink in Italy and Croatia. Bring a refillable bottle. Florence has public drinking fountains (nasoni) everywhere
  • Weather (late May – early June): Italy: 70–85°F, sunny, low rain chance. Dolomites: 55–70°F, cooler at altitude, bring a layer. Croatia: 75–85°F, sunny, great swimming weather. Sunscreen everywhere
  • Packing note: Comfortable walking shoes are essential — Florence cobblestones, Dolomites trails, Venice bridges (300+ with steps), Croatian old towns. One pair of dressier shoes for nice dinners. Quick-dry swimsuit for Croatia
  • For Jenny: Carry a light scarf/shawl — useful for church dress codes (shoulders covered), sun protection, and staying warm in air conditioning or mountain gondolas. Stay hydrated — carry water and electrolytes. Most restaurants are happy to accommodate dietary needs
  • Disc golf: There isn't a strong disc golf scene in these specific destinations, unfortunately. If you find a course near any of the stops, it'd be a bonus — but don't plan around it. Save the disc golf energy for mountain biking in the Dolomites and sea kayaking in Croatia
  • Budget hack: In Italy, eat lunch as your big meal — many restaurants offer pranzo (lunch) menus at lower prices than dinner. In Croatia, konobas (family taverns) are always the best value
  • Tipping: Italy: not expected but €1–2 appreciated. Croatia: 10% at restaurants is standard. Neither country has a strong tipping culture compared to the US
