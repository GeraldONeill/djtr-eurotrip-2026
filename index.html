Prologue: Milan & Cinque Terre

May 15–22  |  Dan & Jenny

Dan & Jenny's Adventure

Two nights in Milan, then down to the Ligurian coast. The plan: fly into Milan, spend a day and a half in the city, then train south to La Spezia as your base for Cinque Terre — hike the legendary coastal trails village to village, eat extraordinary seafood, explore medieval churches and fortresses, then work your way toward Florence via Portovenere and Lucca. Every day has a hike. Every evening has a view. This is your trip.

Sat, May 15 Arrive in Milan Dan & Jenny

Transatlantic flight lands in Milan. Two nights here before heading south to the coast. Don't fight the jet lag — lean into the city instead.

  • Flight: Lufthansa, confirmation AD2APX (Capital One Travel ref: H-M-AD2APX). Arriving at Milan Malpensa (MXP) — Lufthansa transatlantic flights use Malpensa. Take the Malpensa Express train from the airport to Milano Centrale or Cadorna (~45 min). Runs every 30 min €13/pp
  • Staying at: Il Nido 14, Milan — May 15–17 (2 nights). Host: Alberto, +39 333 756 9170. Call him approximately 20 minutes before arrival to arrange check-in. Check-in from 3:00 PM.
  • First stop: Navigli — Milan's canal district. After a transatlantic flight, this is the right amount: outdoor seating along historic canals, aperitivo bars, and a relaxed neighborhood feel. Order a Campari Spritz — Campari was invented in Milan and this is its home $
  • Dinner in Navigli or Brera: Trattoria Masuelli San Marco — a classic Milan trattoria open since 1921. Risotto alla Milanese (saffron risotto) and osso buco are the signatures. Or find any canal-side spot in the Navigli and order whatever looks good. You've earned it $$
Sun, May 16 Milan: The Duomo, the Art & the Aperitivo Dan & Jenny

One full day in one of the world's great cities — design capital, fashion capital, and home to Leonardo's most famous painting.

  • Morning: Duomo di Milano — the fourth-largest cathedral in the world. The interior is extraordinary (free entry). Climb to the rooftop terraces for views over the city and, on clear days, the Alps. Book rooftop access in advance €15–€25/pp rooftop
  • The Last Supper — if booked: Leonardo's Cenacolo at Santa Maria delle Grazie requires reservations made months ahead. If you managed to book, this is the morning to go. If not, the exterior of the church and the nearby Pinacoteca Ambrosiana (which has Leonardo drawings and Raphael cartoons) are excellent alternatives €15/pp if booked
  • Lunch: Brera neighborhood — Milan's art district, cobblestone streets, galleries, and excellent cafes. Latteria di San Marco is a legendary tiny spot — just a few tables, rotating handwritten menu, proper Milanese cooking $$
  • Afternoon: Pinacoteca di Brera — one of Italy's top art museums, right in the heart of the Brera neighborhood. Raphael's Marriage of the Virgin, Mantegna's Dead Christ, Caravaggio. Dan, this is your stop €15/pp
  • Castello Sforzesco — the massive 15th-century Sforza fortress in the city center, with Michelangelo's final unfinished work (the Pietà Rondanini) inside. The castle grounds are free; the museum has a small fee €5/pp museum
  • Aperitivo hour: back to Navigli — Milan invented the aperitivo hour. Every bar puts out free snacks with drinks between 6–9 PM. Order a Negroni or Campari Soda, and eat your way through the bar counter. It's essentially a free dinner if you order a few rounds $
  • Pack up tonight — early train to La Spezia tomorrow morning
Mon, May 17 Travel: Milan → La Spezia & Cinque Terre Dan & Jenny

Check out of Milan and head south. By early afternoon you're in La Spezia — the gateway to Cinque Terre, and your base for the next five nights.

  • Train: Milan Centrale → La Spezia Centrale — direct Intercity trains run several times a day, ~2.5 hours. Book in advance on Trenitalia or Italotreno €20–€35/pp
  • Staying at: Sapri & Colombo Rooms and Apartments, La Spezia — May 17–22 (5 nights). Check-in from 2:00 PM. Booked via Booking.com.
  • About your base: La Spezia is a real Italian city — not a tourist trap — with excellent restaurants, a daily market, and a waterfront promenade. Trains to each of the five CT villages run every 15–30 min from La Spezia Centrale (5–15 min each way). You get the authentic neighborhood feel without the overnight crowds and prices of the villages themselves.
  • Buy a Cinque Terre Card at La Spezia Centrale station or park office. The Train+Trekking Card (~€20/day) includes unlimited train rides between the five villages plus trail access — get this one €20/day or multi-day passes
  • Afternoon: If you arrive with energy, take a quick 12-min train to Monterosso al Mare for your first taste — the largest village, best beach, car-free old town. Or just settle into La Spezia and explore the market and waterfront
  • Dinner in La Spezia: Il Ristoro della Palma — a local spot known for fresh Ligurian seafood, well away from tourist pricing. Or walk the Via del Prione (the main pedestrian street) and find a trattoria that looks right $$
Tue, May 18 Hike: Monterosso → Vernazzo Dan & Jenny

The most challenging section of the Blue Trail (Sentiero Azzurro) — and the most rewarding. Do it first while you're fresh. Steep climbs, narrow paths, and then Vernazzo appears below you like a painting.

  • Morning: Hike Monterosso → Vernazzo (Trail #2, Sentiero Azzurro). ~3.5 km, 1.5–2 hours. Rated strenuous — persistent vertical gain, narrow with high-exposure moments, and lots of stone steps. The views are extraordinary: terraced vineyards dropping into the sea, the coastline stretching in both directions. Bring water and sturdy shoes Included with Cinque Terre Card
  • Arrive in Vernazzo — arguably the most beautiful of the five villages. A natural harbor enclosed by colorful houses stacked up the hillside, with the medieval Doria Castle watching over everything. Walk down to the harbor piazza and take it in
  • Lunch: Ristorante Belforte — inside an 11th-century fortress overlooking the harbor. Spaghetti with mussels and anchovies, eaten in a medieval tower above the Mediterranean. One of those meals you don't forget $$$. GF: Lead with the seafood antipasto platter — it's the highlight anyway. Skip the spaghetti; the grilled whole fish and mixed seafood secondi are all naturally GF. Budget alt: Gianni Franzi — right on the harbor piazza, simple grilled seafood and Ligurian plates. GF-friendly (grilled fish, seafood salad) $–$$
  • Afternoon: Explore Vernazzo. Climb up to Doria Castle for panoramic views of the village and coastline €2. Visit the Church of Santa Margherita d'Antiochia (14th century, built on a rocky promontory). Wander the narrow caruggi (alleyways)
  • Late afternoon: Find a bench at the harbor and watch the fishing boats. Try farinata if you spot it — a thin chickpea-flour flatbread baked in a wood oven, a Ligurian street food that's naturally GF and genuinely delicious. (Focaccia di Recco is the other local classic but it's wheat, so not GF.)
  • Train back to your base (~4 min to Monterosso, covered by your card). Or if you're based in Vernazzo, you're home
  • Dinner: Il Pirata delle Cinque Terre — charming eatery run by twin Sicilian brothers. Friendly atmosphere, great local wine, excellent seafood. Or Lunch Box Vernazzo for incredible sandwiches if you want casual $$
Wed, May 19 Hike: Vernazzo → Corniglia Dan & Jenny

Another spectacular coastal hike, slightly easier than yesterday. Corniglia is the only village not at sea level — it sits on a hilltop ridge 100m above the water, surrounded by vineyards. The quietest, most authentic of the five.

  • Morning: Hike Vernazzo → Corniglia (Trail #2). ~4 km, 1.5–2 hours. Medium difficulty with the highest point on the entire Blue Trail. Steep stone steps but incredible views — terraced hillsides, olive groves, and the sea far below
  • Arrive in Corniglia. (If you took the train, you'll climb the famous Lardarina — 382 brick steps from the station to the village. Or take the shuttle bus.) The village sits on a promontory with 360-degree views. It's the smallest, quietest, and most wine-focused of the five towns
  • Explore Corniglia: Walk the main street (Via Fieschi), find the 14th-century Church of San Pietro with its stunning rose window. Continue to the terrace at the end of Via Fieschi for a panoramic viewpoint that's one of the best in all of Cinque Terre
  • Lunch: Try the local wine — Corniglia produces excellent Sciacchetrà, a sweet dessert wine made from dried grapes on the terraces. Pair it with local cheese at a small bar on Via Fieschi. GF: Skip the focaccia; ask for farinata (chickpea flatbread — naturally GF and a true Ligurian specialty) or a cheese and charcuterie board instead. $–$$
  • Afternoon: Optional extended hike — if you have energy, take the trail from Corniglia up to Volastra (a tiny hilltop hamlet above Manarola) through ancient terraced vineyards. About 1 hour, moderate. Or relax and train back to base
  • Dinner at your base. If Monterosso: L'Ancora della Tortuga — cliff-edge terrace, candlelit, seafood. Romantic and special $$$. Budget alt: Da Eraldo — tucked in the old town, honest Ligurian cooking at local prices. Grilled catch-of-the-day and seafood antipasti are naturally GF $$
Thu, May 20 Hike: Corniglia → Manarola → Via dell'Amore → Riomaggiore Dan & Jenny

The prettiest section of the Blue Trail plus the legendary Via dell'Amore — all in one day. Hike from Corniglia to Manarola, have lunch, then walk the famous cliff path to Riomaggiore for dinner.

  • Morning: Train to Corniglia, then hike Corniglia → Manarola (Trail #2). ~3 km, ~1 hour 15 min. The easiest section of the Blue Trail — relatively gentle with spectacular gardens and sea views the entire way
  • Arrive in Manarola. The village tumbles down a steep ravine to a tiny harbor carved into the rock. Every angle is a postcard. Climb up to the cemetery above town for the iconic looking-back-at-the-village view
  • Lunch: Trattoria Dal Billy — the most celebrated restaurant in Manarola, perched high above the village with stunning views. 12-plate seafood starter, handmade taglierini, and Ligurian white wine. Reserve ahead — books up. $$$. GF: The 12-plate seafood antipasto is the GF move — naturally GF and honestly the best thing on the menu. Dal Billy accommodates. Budget alt: Cappun Magru — casual spot in the village center, fresh seafood plates and fritto misto. Ask for the grilled fish platter (GF) $–$$
  • Afternoon: Nessun Dorma aperitivo — the most famous bar in Cinque Terre, with what may be the best sunset view on the entire Italian coast. Arrive early for a seat $$
  • Via dell'Amore (Manarola → Riomaggiore). ~1 km, ~20 minutes. The most famous path in Cinque Terre — flat, paved, carved into the cliff face above the sea. Reopened Valentine's Day 2025 after 12 years of closure. Reservation required — book through the Cinque Terre National Park website €10 supplement + card
  • Arrive in Riomaggiore — the southernmost village. Houses in every color cascade down to a tiny harbor. Walk down to the marina, explore the castle (13th century, free, good views), peek into galleries on the main street
  • Dinner: Dau Cila — right on the harbor, fresh seafood, candlelit, water lapping below. Or Rio Bistrot for a more modern take. Train back to La Spezia after dinner $$–$$$
Fri, May 21 Portovenere & the Gulf of Poets Dan & Jenny

Your last full day on the Ligurian coast. Portovenere is right out of La Spezia — 15 minutes by ferry or bus — and it's a UNESCO gem that most Cinque Terre visitors miss entirely. Byron, Shelley, and D.H. Lawrence all wrote here. You can see why.

  • Ferry or bus from La Spezia to Portovenere — direct ferry from La Spezia harbor (~15 min) or bus (~30 min). Ferries run regularly in season €5–€10 ferry
  • Explore Portovenere: Walk the colorful waterfront of Calata Doria. Visit the Church of San Pietro — a stunning black-and-white striped Gothic church on a rocky promontory at the edge of the harbor, dating to the 6th century. Views are extraordinary
  • Climb to Doria Castle — the massive Genoese fortress above town. Panoramic views of the harbor, the islands, and the coastline €5
  • Byron's Grotto — the sea cave at the base of the cliffs where Lord Byron reportedly swam. The path down is dramatic
  • Lunch at the harbor: Fresh grilled seafood on the waterfront, local white wine. GF: Skip focaccia; the grilled branzino and mixed seafood platters are all naturally GF. $$
  • Afternoon option: Boat to Palmaria Island — hiking trails, swimming coves, and the Blue Grotto (sea cave). Ferries run frequently from the harbor €5–€8 ferry
  • Or: Lerici half-day — if you've had enough Portovenere by mid-afternoon, hop back to La Spezia and take the 20-min bus to Lerici. Elegant resort town with a 12th-century castle, a palm-lined promenade, and a coastal path to Tellaro — one of Italy's most beautiful tiny villages (no cars, just stone houses and a church on the rocks). Lunch at Locanda Miranda in Tellaro is legendary €5 castle / $$ lunch
  • Dinner back in La Spezia: Osteria della Corte — excellent local spot well away from tourist pricing. A proper Ligurian send-off before tomorrow's move south $$
  • Pack up tonight. Check out tomorrow morning — Lucca and Pisa await
Sat, May 22 Lucca Stopover → Overnight in Pisa Dan & Jenny

Check out of La Spezia and head south with a half-day stop in Lucca — a perfectly preserved Renaissance city enclosed by 16th-century walls you can walk on top of. It's on the way, it's gorgeous, and it's one of Tuscany's most underrated stops. Dan, you're going to love the architecture. Then on to Pisa for the night — Florence tomorrow.

  • Train: La Spezia → Lucca (~1.5 hours, change in Pisa or Viareggio). Leave bags at the station left-luggage if needed €12–€18/pp
  • Walk (or bike) the city walls. Lucca's 16th-century walls are 4 km around, tree-lined, and wide enough for pedestrians and cyclists. The best-preserved Renaissance fortification walls in Italy. Rent bikes if you want to circle the whole thing faster Free to walk / €5–€10 bike rental
  • Explore the historic center: Piazza dell'Anfiteatro — a perfect oval piazza built on top of a Roman amphitheater, ringed with cafes. Torre Guinigi — medieval tower with live oak trees growing on the top. Climb for 360-degree views €5 tower
  • Lunch: Trattoria da Leo — beloved no-frills trattoria packed with locals. Cash preferred $. GF: Skip the tordelli (pasta). Order the grilled meats or stewed beans from the secondi. Or try Ristorante Giglio on Piazza del Giglio, which accommodates dietary restrictions well.
  • Train: Lucca → Pisa — ~30 min, regional train. Trains not yet booked
  • Check in to Helvetia Pisa Tower — Via Don Gaetano Boschi 31, 56126 Pisa. Check-in from 2:00 PM. Booking.com confirmation 6803675421, PIN 7344. Phone: +39 050 622 2706. No elevator (stairs only). City tax: €2/pp/night. Check-out 8:30–10:30 AM
  • Evening: Stroll to the Piazza dei Miracoli — the Leaning Tower, the Cathedral, and the Baptistery are all floodlit at night and stunning without the daytime crowds. The hotel is a 5-min walk away
  • Dinner: Osteria dei Cavalieri — excellent Tuscan trattoria in the university quarter, well-priced and locally loved $$. GF: Grilled meats and fish secondi are safe; skip the pasta courses. They accommodate dietary requests.

Sun, May 24 Renaissance Day All Four

This is Dan's day. Florence is the birthplace of the Renaissance, and this is where you soak it in — the Uffizi, the Duomo, the David. Build the day around art and history, with food and wine filling the gaps.

  • 8:15 AM: Uffizi Gallery — Botticelli's Birth of Venus, Caravaggio, Leonardo, Raphael. Timed entry at 8:15, so arrive a few minutes early. Allow 2–3 hours. View tickets (PDF) · Booking confirmation €29/pp · Booking P1ADV129
  • Mid-morning: Duomo complex — a casual stroll, not a deep dive (plenty of museum time today). What's free:

    • Admire the exterior facades — the Cathedral's polychrome marble, Giotto's Campanile, and the octagonal Baptistery are spectacular from the piazza

Look for the cheese and charcuterie stalls, the grilled meat counter, and the ribollita-free vegetable stalls. Most vendors can plate something naturally GF on request.

  • Dinner: Buca Mario — Florentine bistecca (T-bone steak), rustic cellar atmosphere, one of the oldest restaurants in Florence (1886). Share the bistecca alla fiorentina for the table. Also incredible ribollita $$$. GF: The bistecca alla fiorentina is one of the most naturally GF dishes in the world — just beef, salt, olive oil, and fire. This is the move. Budget alt: Trattoria Mario — communal tables near San Lorenzo, pure Florentine cooking, loud and packed with locals. Grilled meats and roasted vegetables are GF; skip the soups (bread-thickened) $

Also ask about Amaro dell'Erborista — small-batch, family-recipe Amari from Tuscan herbalists. Many trattorias will have their own house Amaro made from secret recipes. Mon, May 25 Oltrarno, Artisans & Tuscan Vibes All Four

Cross the Arno to the Oltrarno — Florence's bohemian left bank. This is the artisan quarter: leather workshops, paper marbling studios, quiet piazzas with real Florentine neighborhood energy. And this afternoon: you're making pasta together.

Jenny can take little tastes — Amaro is served in small pours anyway $$

Quick Tips

  • Book museums and restaurants NOW. Uffizi, Accademia, and the Duomo dome climb sell out weeks ahead. Same for top restaurants — Osteria alle Testiere, Trattoria Sostanza, and Nautika all need reservations
  • Trains in Italy: Book on Trainline or Trenitalia 2–3 months ahead for the best prices. Frecciarossa (red) is the fast train. Italo (maroon) is the competing fast train. Both are great
  • Croatia ferries: Book catamaran tickets on Ferryhopper at least a week ahead in summer. They fill up, especially Split→Hvar and Hvar→Dubrovnik
  • Money: Italy and Croatia both use the Euro. Cards are accepted almost everywhere, but carry some cash for market vendors, small trattorias, and tips
  • Chase Sapphire: Use it for flights (transfer points to airline partners for better value), hotels via the Chase travel portal, and as your primary card abroad — no foreign transaction fees, trip protection built in
  • Water: Tap water is safe to drink in Italy and Croatia. Bring a refillable bottle. Florence has public drinking fountains (nasoni) everywhere
  • Weather (late May – early June): Italy: 70–85°F, sunny, low rain chance. Dolomites: 55–70°F, cooler at altitude, bring a layer. Croatia: 75–85°F, sunny, great swimming weather. Sunscreen everywhere
  • Packing note: Comfortable walking shoes are essential — Florence cobblestones, Dolomites trails, Venice bridges (300+ with steps), Croatian old towns. One pair of dressier shoes for nice dinners. Quick-dry swimsuit for Croatia
  • Church dress codes: Shoulders and knees must be covered in Italian churches (including the Duomo, St. Mark's, etc.). Carry a light scarf or shawl — doubles as sun protection and a layer for mountain gondolas
  • Disc golf: There isn't a strong disc golf scene in these specific destinations, unfortunately. If you find a course near any of the stops, it'd be a bonus — but don't plan around it. Save the disc golf energy for mountain biking in the Dolomites and sea kayaking in Croatia
  • Budget hack: In Italy, eat lunch as your big meal — many restaurants offer pranzo (lunch) menus at lower prices than dinner. In Croatia, konobas (family taverns) are always the best value
  • Tipping: Italy: not expected but €1–2 appreciated. Croatia: 10% at restaurants is standard. Neither country has a strong tipping culture compared to the US
